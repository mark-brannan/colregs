## Comment 1 — Corpus identity needs a legal-instrument/version layer

**Priority:** must-resolve-before-schema-freeze
**Sources:** 02 (§2, §10, closing "one architectural change"). No overlap with 01.

> The corpus identity is `(jurisdiction × language × source)`, and the provenance fields (source, retrieved, licence) are good — but "source" is carrying too much. COLREGS is not static: there's the 1972 Convention, the amendment rounds (1981, 1987, ..., 2013 Rule 27 exemptions expiring, etc.), consolidated editions, and national implementing texts that amend on their own schedule. Nothing in the proposed model distinguishes "UNTS original English" from "IMO consolidated English as amended through 2013" — both would legitimately claim `jurisdiction=intl, language=en, tier=authentic/official`, with different text at the same paragraph paths.
>
> The ADR is currently a three-layer model (semantic skeleton → text corpus → display catalog). I think it's missing a layer between the first two: the **legal instrument/version** — instrument identity, jurisdiction, amendment state, effective/publication dates — with corpora attaching to *that* rather than directly to `(jurisdiction × language)`. You don't need to populate it now; you need the identity concept to exist so the first two corpora don't get landed against an implicit "the current text" that stops being unique the moment an amendment or a second edition shows up.
>
> Since REQ-LANG-3 is the line that would freeze this ("corpus per (jurisdiction × language × source)"), it's worth deciding before the schema lands rather than after translations attach. Is the intent that "source" already *is* the version identity (i.e., a new edition = a new source = a new corpus), and if so, should REQ-LANG-3 say that explicitly — including what happens when two corpora share tier + language but differ in amendment state?

## Comment 2 — Paragraph paths: stable identifier or source-specific citation?

**Priority:** must-resolve-before-schema-freeze
**Sources:** 02 (§3, §16). No overlap with 01.

> The design keys everything — citation, composition, corpus text, coverage manifests — on paragraph path (`27(a)(i)`). That's elegant, and it works today because there is exactly one skeleton. But a paragraph path is a *legal citation*, not an immutable identifier, and the two roles pull apart under amendment: an inserted paragraph renumbers its siblings, a split turns one path into two, and a national implementation can number differently from the treaty. At that point `27(a)(i)` in one corpus and `27(a)(i)` in another are different provisions wearing the same key, and every cross-reference in `applicability.json` silently follows the wrong one.
>
> Related: REQ-LANG-3 keys corpora "by paragraph path" and implicitly assumes every corpus has exactly one text value per skeleton paragraph. Real translations reorder clauses, merge or split paragraphs, and carry translator notes. Even if the answer is "we don't support that," the constraint "a corpus entry MAY contain zero or one text value per skeleton paragraph" should be a stated decision, not an accident of the file shape.
>
> Two ways out: (a) introduce a stable internal `paragraph_id` distinct from the displayed `citation_path`, or (b) declare explicitly that paragraph paths are *source-version-specific* and accept that they don't survive legal revisions — which then leans further on the instrument/version question raised in the other thread. Which of the two is the intended contract? (a) is more work now; (b) is honest but makes cross-corpus alignment a consumer problem forever.

## Comment 3 — The tier taxonomy conflates legal status with translation provenance, and "authentic" needs a stricter definition

**Priority:** must-resolve-before-schema-freeze
**Sources:** 02 (§6, §11) primarily; 01 (§3 `authority_tier`) proposes essentially the same single-axis taxonomy the ADR already has, so 01's version is superseded rather than merged — kept 02's two-axis framing.

> The `authentic / official / national / community` ladder reads as one axis but is measuring two different things. `authentic`/`official`/`national` describe **legal standing of the source**; `community` describes **who produced the translation**. Those cross: a community member can transcribe (not translate) an authentic UNTS text; a national authority can publish a translation with no legal force in its own courts. REQ-LANG-8's review gate is really about translation provenance, not legal tier — a community *transcription* of authentic text has very different risk than a community *translation*, yet both land in the same tier.
>
> Suggest splitting into two fields, e.g. `legal_status: authentic | official | national | unofficial` and something like `text_origin: source-original | official-translation | community-translation | community-transcription`, with REQ-LANG-8's named-human-review gate keyed to the origin, not the tier.
>
> Separately: "authentic" is a legal term of art and should be defined by the instrument, not by the repo's assessment — "text identified by the Convention's final clauses as equally authentic," not "the original text." The ADR already flags (Q-6) that the treaty-language facts are recalled, not verified, which is the right instinct; the tier table's examples column ("en and fr per the Convention") shouldn't harden into schema-level truth before Q-6 closes. Does splitting the axis feel like over-engineering for the four cases in view, or does the community-transcription-of-authentic-text case already break the single ladder for you?

## Comment 4 — Partial coverage semantics: "missing" is three different states, and mixed-language results need a stated rule

**Priority:** must-resolve-before-schema-freeze
**Sources:** 02 (§7, §9). No overlap with 01.

> REQ-LANG-5 gets the big thing right — coverage is declared, silence never implies it. But the coverage contract itself is underspecified. When a corpus manifest omits `27(d)`, that could mean: (1) not yet translated, (2) the source document genuinely doesn't contain this paragraph (a national text that dropped it), or (3) the paragraph doesn't exist in that jurisdiction's skeleton at all. Those are materially different — (1) invites a consumer to fall back, (2) is legal information in its own right — and a bare list of present paths can't distinguish them.
>
> The downstream version of the same gap: a consumer asks for Finnish, the Finnish corpus has `27(a)–(c)`, English has all four. Can they render three paragraphs in Finnish and one in English? The design technically leaves that to the consumer, but for legal text that's a dangerous silence. A rule along the lines of "a resolved view MUST identify the corpus used for every textual unit; a mixed-corpus result MUST NOT be represented as a single authoritative translation" costs one sentence in REQ-LANG and prevents a real class of downstream misrepresentation — without violating the package's no-fallback principle, since it constrains *labeling*, not *selection*.
>
> Would you extend the coverage manifest to a per-path status (present / absent-from-source / untranslated) or keep it as a bare path list and record absent-from-source somewhere else (the skeleton's per-jurisdiction gaps)?

## Comment 5 — REQ-LANG-7 is stronger than it needs to be, in the wrong direction

**Priority:** should-resolve-before-first-translation
**Sources:** both — 01 (§8 fallback chains, modular packaging) and 02 (§8). They partially **conflict**: 01 recommends the package define an explicit fallback resolution chain (`es-MX → es → en`), which is exactly what 02 calls the "bad" version and what REQ-LANG-7 already forbids. Kept 02's framing (consistent with the package's own philosophy) and salvaged 01's packaging point, which is orthogonal and survives.

> REQ-LANG-7 — "The package MUST NOT encode a language fallback policy" — has the right instinct but bans slightly the wrong thing. What actually must never happen is the package **silently substituting a different legal corpus** because the requested language is unavailable. That's narrower and stronger than banning all fallback-related data. As written, REQ-LANG-7 would also forbid genuinely useful, non-prescriptive metadata: e.g. a Finnish national corpus declaring that Sweden's Swedish text is the legally cognate sibling, or per-corpus coverage stats that let a consumer implement fallback *safely*. Declaring relationships is describing the world; choosing for the consumer is policy. The current wording conflates them.
>
> Suggested rewording: "The package MUST NOT silently substitute one corpus for another, and MUST NOT prescribe a fallback order; it MAY declare relationships between corpora (translation_of, cognate national texts) and MUST expose enough metadata — coverage, tier, source — for consumers to implement fallback deliberately."
>
> Related but separable: since consumers include embedded targets (SignalK on a Pi), it's worth confirming that the file-per-corpus / file-per-catalog layout in the sketch is a commitment, not a convenience — one file per language is what makes "load only what you need" possible without a bundler, which feels like a REQ-PKG concern that currently isn't written down. Is the per-file split meant to be normative, or could a future consolidation legitimately merge them?

## Comment 6 — Display catalogs get almost no provenance, and no terminology anchor

**Priority:** should-resolve-before-first-translation
**Sources:** both — 01 (§3 provenance, §4 glossary) and 02 (§5). Overlap: both demand translation provenance; 01 aims it at legal text (already covered by REQ-LANG-3/8), 02 correctly notices the *catalogs* are the uncovered surface — kept 02's target and 01's glossary mechanism.

> REQ-LANG-8's named-human-review gate applies to `community`-tier *corpora*. Display catalogs — per REQ-LANG-6 a separate artifact — appear to have no provenance requirements at all. But this is maritime safety vocabulary, not app chrome: `light.masthead`, `not_under_command`, `restricted_in_ability_to_manoeuvre` have legally defined meanings, and a wrong catalog label in a training app teaches the wrong rule. Who translated `fi.json`, who reviewed it, when, and against which source terminology should be recorded — not the full legal-corpus provenance model, just a lightweight header (`contributors`, `reviewers`, `last_reviewed`, `source`, `license`).
>
> The "against which terminology" part is the sharper half. Terms like give-way/stand-on recur across catalog labels, rule text, and eventually image captions; two contributors translating different files independently will drift (*navire privilégié* vs *navire non privilégié* is a classic inversion hazard). The corpora can't be the anchor — they're verbatim and may not even exist yet in that language. A small per-language concept glossary (concept key → sanctioned term), which CI can check catalog values against, would keep a language internally consistent as it grows across PRs.
>
> Is extending REQ-LANG-8's review gate to catalogs acceptable to you contribution-cost-wise, or is the catalog deliberately being kept low-ceremony to make "a single additive PR" true — and if so, where does terminology consistency get enforced instead?

## Comment 7 — `en-US` in the corpus identity is doing three jobs

**Priority:** should-resolve-before-first-translation
**Sources:** both — 01 (§1) and 02 (§1). Heavy overlap; 01's core point (separate locale from jurisdiction) is already the ADR's central decision, so 01 adds little here — what survives is 02's second-order observation that the *BCP 47 tag itself* is now being overloaded. Kept 02's framing entirely.

> The ADR gets language-vs-jurisdiction right, and REQ-LANG-1's "neither MUST ever be inferred from the other" is exactly the right rule. But the sketch filename `intl.en-US.uscg.json` shows the same conflation reappearing one level down, inside the BCP 47 tag. This corpus isn't an "en-US localization" of the international rules — it's a USCG-produced English-language national rendition. `en-US` here is smuggling in the *source jurisdiction* (US) under the guise of a *language subtag*. The awkward questions arrive quickly: is a Finnish government translation `fi` or `fi-FI`? Is UK-published English a different language tag or a different source? If someone requests `en`, does `en-US` match?
>
> The fix is cheap because the tier/source metadata already exists: use the minimal sufficient BCP 47 tag (`en`, `fi`) for the language field, and let source jurisdiction live where it already does — in the source/tier metadata. Reserve region subtags for cases where the *language variety itself* is the distinguishing fact, if any ever arise. It's worth adding one sentence to REQ-LANG-1 generalizing its own principle: no semantic property — legal applicability, source jurisdiction, tier — may be inferred from the BCP 47 tag.
>
> Was `en-US` in the sketch a deliberate claim that US spelling is corpus-distinguishing (defensible, given the maneuver/manoeuvre observation in the ADR itself), or a convenience label? If the former, how do you stop region subtags from becoming a shadow jurisdiction field?

## Comment 8 — "Verbatim" needs a byte-level definition, and "source" needs structure

**Priority:** should-resolve-before-first-translation
**Sources:** 02 (§12, §13, §14, §15, §18) plus 01 (§6 canonical units, §7 UTF-8/RTL — folded in as minor supporting points; 01's `dir` metadata recommendation dropped as a presentation concern the ADR correctly excludes).

> REQ-LANG-3 inherits REQ-MODEL-1's verbatim rule per corpus, and the ADR says Arabic "carries fine" in JSON strings. Agreed on both — but "verbatim" is undefined at the level where it will actually be tested. Is `é` the same as `e` + combining acute? If a corpus is ever checked against a source hash, NFC-vs-NFD is the difference between pass and fail. And for RTL text specifically, well-meaning tooling loves to insert bidi control characters to "fix" the rendering of embedded `27(a)` / `22.5°` fragments — which silently makes the text non-verbatim. Suggest two explicit rules: (1) state the normalization policy (e.g. "verbatim means the extracted text NFC-normalized, with an extraction hash recorded"), and (2) the data layer MUST NOT insert or strip Unicode bidi/control characters, and localization (digits, separators, units, quotation marks) MUST NOT be applied to text inside a legal corpus. The structured numerics (`arc_deg`, ranges) already stay locale-neutral, which is right.
>
> Adjacent, same theme of "provenance that software can check": corpus-level source metadata should be structured (`publisher`, `title`, `identifier`, `edition`, `publication_date`, `effective_date`, `retrieved`, `url`) rather than prose — and the licence field probably needs to distinguish source-text rights from the repo's redistribution rights, since a translator can hold rights in a translation of a public-domain treaty (directly relevant to Q-7's sequencing role). Finally, since the ADR says filename is convenience and in-file metadata is authoritative: that's only safe if CI verifies they agree, or `intl.fi.finlex.json` will one day contain `language: sv`.
>
> Do you want to pin the normalization policy in REQ-LANG now, or defer it to the ADR that lands the first corpus split — accepting that the existing `rules.json` text was extracted without one?
